## 2. What happens: the dipole flips

At fixed total strain the stress is

$$\sigma_{xz}=G_U\,(\gamma-\gamma_p),\qquad \gamma_p=\frac{b\,\Delta s}{L_xL_z} \tag{1}$$

Here $\Delta s$ is the change of the dipole separation along $x$. The 2-ps averages at the 20-ps period give $\gamma_p =
\gamma-\sigma/G_U$, which switches between about +0.015 and −0.015 every half cycle, every cycle over 200 ps. That swing
is 1.5× the imposed strain. It corresponds to $\Delta s \approx 14$ Å: the pair jumps between the two stable dipole
configurations of the periodic cell. The stress is then opposite to the strain over most of the cycle ($\sigma \approx
-1.0$ GPa at $\gamma = +0.0098$). Unforced, the pilot already wandered by ±11 Å (35–80 core atoms, pair gliding through the
whole cell in ~10 ps). At 4000 K this cell has an almost flat dipole energy, and $G_U\gamma_0 = 1.8$ GPa is far above the
switching stress. This is the bistable, overdriven regime, not Eq. 3 of the plan.

## 3. 0.2 ps: an estimate of B

At the shortest period the response is closest to linear ($|\sigma_3/\sigma_1| = 0.02$). With $k \approx 0$ (free glide),
plan Eq. 3 becomes a Maxwell element:

$$\frac{G'}{G''}=\omega\tau,\qquad \tau=\frac{B}{\rho b^2G_U} \tag{2}$$

The measured values give $\omega\tau = 2.77$, so $\tau = 0.088$ ps and $B \approx 9\times10^{-5}$ Pa s. The driven glide
value is $1.76\times10^{-4}$ Pa s, a factor of 2 away. However:
- The fit is not self-consistent: $|G^*| = 122$ GPa, against the 168 GPa implied by $G_U = 180$.
- The implied glide speed is about 5 km/s, ~0.8 of the shear-wave speed (~6 km/s). That is the relativistic drag regime.

So this is an order-of-magnitude check only.

## 4. Consequence and next run

A linear dislocation $G^*(\omega)$ cannot be extracted from this cell at $\gamma_0 = 0.01$. RC (11-22) chose the
equilibrium route: **job 7718928** (`Dislocations/M1_edge001_4000K_GK`, P100, 24 h). It starts from the pilot
`eq.data`, runs 5 ps NVT, then 1 ns NVE. It writes the stress every 2 steps and the trajectory every 0.5 ps.

At fixed strain in a solid, the stress fluctuations lower the modulus from the affine (Born) value:

$$C(t)=\langle\delta\sigma_{xz}(0)\,\delta\sigma_{xz}(t)\rangle,\qquad G^*(\omega)=G_B-\frac{V}{k_BT}\Big[C(0)-i\omega\int_0^\infty C(t)\,e^{-i\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big] \tag{3}$$

The phonon part of $C$ decays in ~0.1 ps and gives $G_U \approx 180$ GPa. A slow part from dislocation glide adds a
Debye term:

$$\Delta G=\frac{V\,C_{slow}(0)}{k_BT},\qquad G^*(\omega)\approx G_U-\frac{\Delta G}{1+i\omega\tau},\qquad C_{slow}(t)=C_{slow}(0)\,e^{-t/\tau} \tag{4}$$

Comparing Eq. 4 with plan Eq. 3 gives $k$ and $B$. Free glide ($k\to0$) would give $\Delta G \to G_U$, i.e. a slow stress
noise of $\sqrt{G_Uk_BT/V} \approx 0.6$ GPa rms. The plastic strain from the core separation (Eq. 1) is a check.

The alternative, not run, was $\gamma_0 = 0.001$ at 0.2 and 1 ps.

Neither changes the scaling argument for the Earth. B sets $\tau_L$ (plan Eq. 4), and with $B \sim 10^{-4}$ Pa s the drag
(Granato–Lücke) peak lies at ~1 MHz–10 GHz for $L$ = 10–0.1 µm ($C = G_Ub^2/2$). The seismic band needs the thermally activated (kink-pair,
unpinning) terms.

The slow xz part is 5.6× the free-glide value $G_U \approx 180$ GPa.

For relative glide $\Delta s$ against only the cell (image) stiffness, Eq. 1 gives a harmonic energy
$\tfrac12 V G_U (b\,\Delta s/A)^2$, so

$$\frac{V\langle\delta\sigma_{xz}^2\rangle}{k_BT}=G_U\quad\text{(free glide)},\qquad
\frac{V\langle\delta\sigma_{xz}^2\rangle}{k_BT}=\frac{G_U}{1-\kappa_d/\kappa_{im}} \tag{5}$$

Here $\kappa_d$ is the magnitude of the negative curvature of the dipole interaction in the separation and
$\kappa_{im}$ the image stiffness. The measured ratio gives $\kappa_d/\kappa_{im} \approx 0.82$.

**Cause: the cell geometry.** `build_dislocation_dipole.py` places the edge dipole *vertically*, with both cores at the
same $x$ (h = Lz/2 = 30 Å apart) and no tilt. For an edge dipole that is the unstable configuration; the stable ones
are near ±45°. The cell's image stiffness holds the separation near 0. The isolated-pair estimate of the
destabilising curvature, $L_xL_z/(2\pi(1-\nu)h^2) \approx 0.6$ of $\kappa_{im}$, is close to the measured 0.82. The
net is a shallow double well at $\Delta s \approx \pm 7$ Å, which is the ±0.015 plastic strain the sweep flipped
between. The relaxed fixed-strain modulus $G_U - 1019$ GPa < 0 says the same thing.

None of this is dislocation physics of MgO; it is the cell.

**Fix:** build the edge dipole in the quadrupolar arrangement, as already done for the screw: cores at $(x_c, z_1)$ and
$(x_c + L_x/2,\ z_1 + L_z/2)$, with the plastic strain of the separation carried in the tilt $xz = b/2$ (Bulatov & Cai
2006 §5.5). That is a symmetric minimum. The test of success is Eq. 5: $V\langle\delta\sigma^2\rangle/k_BT \le G_U$.
The bridgmanite B1 cell was built the same way (vertical edge dipole), so it needs the same fix.

## 8. The 1-ps anomaly: a resonant standing wave from `remap v` (11-22, RC: "Look into the MgO 1 ps anomaly")

**Symptom.** The M1q 1 ps run (and the vertical M1 1 ps run) has G″ < 0. Its PE is 23–37 eV above the 5/20 ps runs and
still rising, and P is 2–4 GPa lower. The cores are intact and stay at $L_x/2$ separation. The perfect cell at 1 ps is
normal.

**Diagnosis.** The non-affine x displacement field from the trajectory, in fractional coordinates so that the imposed
affine shear drops out, shows the effect. In the 1 ps run it carries a coherent standing wave at the drive frequency:

$$u_x(z,t) \approx U\sin(2\pi z/L_z)\,\cos(\omega t+\phi),\qquad U = 1.5\text{–}1.8\ \text{Å} \tag{6}$$

It grows within ~5 ps and stays at that amplitude for 200 ps. At 5 and 20 ps the drive-frequency component is ≤ 0.1 Å.

The lowest transverse standing wave of the cell lies at

$$f_n = \frac{n\,c_s}{L_z},\qquad c_s=\sqrt{G_U/\rho}=\sqrt{179.4\ \text{GPa}/5004\ \text{kg m}^{-3}} = 5.99\ \text{km/s} \tag{7}$$

| cell | $L_z$ (Å) | $f_1$ (THz) |
|---|---|---|
| dipole cells (vertical and quad) | 60.29 | 0.993 (period 1.007 ps): on resonance with the 1 ps drive |
| perfect MgO | 30.14 | 1.99 |
| B1q bridgmanite ($c_{66}$ 256 GPa) | 36.79 | ≈ 1.9 |

Why `remap v` drives the wave: under `fix deform remap v` + `nvt/sllod`, the streaming profile $u = \dot\gamma z$ is
not accelerated when $\dot\gamma$ changes. The momentum enters only through the velocity remap at the periodic z
boundary, so the peculiar velocity has a sawtooth error with a jump at $z = 0$. The n = 1 term of a sawtooth is
$\sin(2\pi z/L_z)$, which is exactly the measured profile: nodes at 0 and $L_z/2$. Without resonance the error is only
$\gamma_0L_z/\pi = 0.1$ Å; on resonance it grows ~17×.

How this produces the symptoms:
- **Mean stress:** in a homogeneous linear crystal the wave averages out of the mean stress, so the perfect cell is
  unaffected. In the dipole cell the cores respond to it.
- **G″ < 0:** the energy enters through the remap, not as $\oint\bar\sigma\,d\gamma$.
- **PE and P:** the thermostat counts the wave's kinetic energy as heat, so the true thermal T is lower. That gives the
  PE and P shifts.

**Test, job 7848423** (`Dislocations/M1q_remapx_test`, README). The same start and drive, but `REMAP x` (new option in
`tools/anelastic_sweep.lammps`: affine position remap every step plus plain `fix nvt`). Result:
- **the wave is gone**: the drive-frequency $\sin(2\pi z/L_z)$ amplitude is 0.01–0.19 Å, against 0.6–1.8 Å with remap v;
- PE is flat at −18359 eV, close to the 5/20 ps runs (−18354), not −18320;
- the response is linear ($|\sigma_3/\sigma_1|$ = 0.02);
- **G′ = 146 ± 9, G″ = +135 ± 10 GPa** (30 ps, 3 blocks; 5-ps windows give G″ 107–172).

**Control, job 7850736.** The perfect cell (4096 atoms) at 1 ps with `REMAP x`, γ₀ 0.01, 20 ps, gives G′ = 179.8 ± 0.9
and G″ = −0.7 ± 0.6 GPa. That is identical to its `remap v` value (179.8 / −0.7), so `remap x` adds no artifact in the
perfect crystal. The dipole's G″ = 135 GPa at 1 ps is therefore the dislocation response of this cell.

That G″ exceeds the Debye maximum $\Delta G/2 \approx 48$ GPa (with $\Delta G \approx 97$ from 20 ps). This points to an
underdamped resonance of the relative glide in the image well:
- per unit length, $k \approx G_Ub^2/(L_xL_z) = 5\times10^8$ Pa and $m \approx \rho b^2\ln(R/r_0)/4\pi \approx 6\times10^{-17}$ kg/m;
- with reduced mass $m/2$, $f_0 \approx 0.7$ THz;
- $Q = m\omega_0/B \approx 1.4$ for $B = 1.76\times10^{-4}$ Pa s.

These are estimates, not a fit. The resonance is set by the cell (image stiffness), so it is not an Earth mechanism. In
the Earth the line tension of pinned segments takes this role (Granato–Lücke, MHz–GHz).

**Consequences:**
- MgO 5 and 20 ps (`remap v`) are free of the wave.
- The 0.2 ps points are doubtful in all cells. The dipole cell's n = 5 mode is at ≈ 4.8 THz, and the perfect cell at
  0.2 ps has G″ = −5.0 GPa and a PE 63 eV low.
- B1q at 1 ps is off resonance ($f_1$ ≈ 1.9 THz), but its extra softening may be the same effect, weaker.
- Use `REMAP x` for all further oscillatory sweeps. The default is still `v`, so the old runs are reproducible.

**Source check** (LAMMPS 22 Jul 2025 update 2, `src/fix_nvt_sllod.cpp` lines 115–128). The velocity update is
$v \leftarrow v\,f_\eta - \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}(\dot h h^{-1})\,c$. Here $c$ is the velocity with the temp/deform bias removed
(for `psllod yes`, the bias is removed after the product instead). Then the bias is restored, so $v$ stays the lab
velocity. In neither variant is there a term in $\mathrm d(\dot h h^{-1})/\mathrm dt$. When the strain rate changes, the
streaming profile is therefore accelerated only by the remap at the periodic boundary. `psllod yes` would not cure the
standing wave; `remap x` does.
