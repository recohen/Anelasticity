# Bridgmanite (MgSiO₃) Rheology — Literature Map

Oct 12, 2026 · @Ron

Direct high-T bridgmanite deformation data exist only up to \~27 GPa and down to \~10⁻⁶ s⁻¹; theory (mostly the Lille group) fills in the rest. The two camps now disagree on what deforms the mantle: dislocation creep, diffusion creep, or diffusion-controlled pure climb.

## Overview — where the field stands

- **Mechanism at mantle conditions.** Dislocation creep: Tsujino et al. 2022, Guan et al. 2026. Diffusion creep: Karato et al. 1995, Ammann et al. 2010. Pure climb creep: Boioli et al. 2017, Reali et al. 2019, Cordier et al. 2023.
- **Dominant slip system.** 001 at 25 GPa, 1873 K (Tsujino 2016); T-switch 010 → 100 near 1800 K (Guan 2026); P-switch (100) → (010) between 24 and 50 GPa (Gay 2024; theory: Hirel 2014, Kraych 2016).
- **Bdg vs Fp in aggregates.** Fp-weak, strain-localizing (Girard 2016, Nzogang 2018) vs Bdg load-bearing (Xu F. 2022, Cordier 2023).
- **Grain size and the \~1000 km viscosity jump.** Grain-size driven (Fei 2023) vs grain-size-insensitive dislocation creep (Guan 2026) vs fabric-driven seismic discontinuity (Magali 2026).
- **Gap.** No high-T flow-law data above \~30 GPa; Al, Fe³⁺ and water effects on creep are barely measured.

A five-paper entry path: Tsujino 2022 (flow law), Kraych 2016 (glide theory), Boioli 2017 (climb creep), Fei 2023 (grain size), Guan 2026 (slip transition). Entries marked † were cited from memory and not opened; check them before citing.

## Experiments — creep laws, diffusion, grain growth, two-phase aggregates

The only in-situ steady-state flow law for bridgmanite is Tsujino et al. (2022); everything else at mantle conditions is inferred from Si diffusion plus grain size. Grain size is now the central lever: Bdg-rich rocks coarsen fast, pushing them toward dislocation creep and higher viscosity.

## Theory — dislocation cores, lattice friction, glide

The Lille group's chain (DFT γ-surfaces → Peierls–Nabarro → atomistic cores → kink-pair mobility) predicts 100 and 010 as easiest, with lattice friction so high that \~1 GPa is still needed to glide at mantle strain rates. That result motivates the climb-creep models in the next section.

## Theory — point defects, diffusion, climb creep, bulk viscosity

If glide is frozen out, strain must come from diffusion: either classic diffusion creep (grain-size sensitive) or pure climb creep (grain-size insensitive, no CPO). Both put the viscosity on Si diffusivity and vacancy concentration, which is where first-principles work has the most leverage.

## Grain boundaries — Bdg boundary motion and Bdg–Fp interphase boundaries

Grain-boundary work on bridgmanite is thin. Experiments constrain grain growth (Zener-pinned in Bdg + Fp) and grain-boundary diffusion; atomistic studies of boundary mobility exist mostly for MgO. I found no published atomistic or DFT study of the MgSiO₃|MgO interphase boundary itself (structure, energy, sliding or migration) — an open target.
