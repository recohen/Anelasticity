# Temperature dependence of loss and transport in MgO and MgSiO₃: results (2026-10-13)

**Question (RC, 2026-10-12):** for both systems, is the T dependence an activated process with one activation energy, or possibly T-independent?

For each quantity y(T) the tool computes:

$$E_{loc}=s\,k_B\,\frac{\Delta\ln y}{\Delta(1/T)},\qquad s=+1\ (\eta,\zeta),\quad s=-1\ (D,\ Q^{-1}) \tag{1}$$

$$\ln y=a+s\,\frac{E}{k_BT}\quad\text{(single activation energy)} \tag{2}$$

$$\ln y=a+n\ln T\quad(n=0:\ \text{T-independent}) \tag{3}$$

$$\ln y=a+s\,\frac{B}{T-T_0}\quad\text{(VFT, 4 or more points)} \tag{4}$$

How to read the tests:
- A single activation energy means $E_{loc}$ is constant within errors and Eq. 2 fits with $\chi^2/\nu\approx1$.
- A T-independent quantity has $n\approx0$ in Eq. 3.

All energies are isochoric. The isobaric energy is larger by about 0.2–0.4 eV:

$$E_P=E_V+k_BT^2\left(\frac{\partial\ln\eta}{\partial p}\right)_T\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\right)_V \tag{5}$$

For the vacancy cell, the vacancy jump rate follows from the tracer D of species i:

$$\Gamma_v\approx\frac{6\,D_i\,N_i}{n_v\,\ell^2} \tag{6}$$

The inputs are:
- $N_i$: atoms of species i, 250 Mg and 762 O;
- $n_v$ = 6 vacancies per species;
- ℓ: the jump length, taken from the pseudo-cubic edge $a_p=(5V)^{1/3}$ = 3.20 Å. ℓ = $a_p$ for Mg and $a_p/\sqrt2$ = 2.26 Å for O.

The correlation factor is set to 1, so $\Gamma_v$ is a lower bound by a factor of order 1.

### 2.2 Perfect crystals at THz: not activated

| crystal | channel, f | $Q^{-1}$ (low T → high T) | $E_{loc}$ (eV) | Eq. 3: n, χ²/ν |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MgO perfect B1, 3000/4000/5000 K | uniaxial 1 THz | 0.0202, 0.0295, 0.0374 | 0.39 ± 0.05, 0.41 ± 0.08 | **1.22 ± 0.11**, 0.7 |
| MgO perfect B1 | uniaxial 0.2 THz | 0.0132, 0.0140, 0.0142 | 0.07 ± 0.17, 0.02 ± 0.33 | **0.16 ± 0.38**, 0.0 |
| MgO perfect B1 | shear (C₄₄) 1 THz | 0.0002, 0.0022, 0.0028 | 2.5 ± 4.7, 0.4 ± 2.0 | unresolved |
| bridgmanite perfect, 2000–5000 K | uniaxial 1 THz | 0.0057 → 0.0159 | 0.20 → 0.77 | **0.98 ± 0.14**, 0.6 |
| bridgmanite perfect | shear 1 THz | 0.0064 → 0.0159 | 0.24 → 0.98 | **0.95 ± 0.36**, 0.2 |

- **MgO, new result.** The perfect-B1 uniaxial loss at 1 THz rises as $T^{1.2}$. A formal Arrhenius energy is 0.40 ± 0.03 eV, constant over 3000–5000 K. That is 5–10× below any atomic barrier in MgO (2.6–3.8 eV, §5b), so it is not an activation energy but phonon (anharmonic) damping. Over a factor 1.7 in T a power law and an Arrhenius form cannot be told apart. The physics decides.
- **MgO uniaxial loss at 0.2 THz is T-independent** ($n$ = 0.2 ± 0.4) at $Q^{-1}$ ≈ 0.014. This is what Akhiezer damping gives for ωτ_ph ≪ 1. There $Q^{-1}\propto\omega\,\tau_{ph}\,C_V T$, and with $\tau_{ph}\propto1/T$ the T dependence cancels. This interpretation is plausible but has not been checked independently.
- **MgO C₄₄ shear loss is unresolved** at all three temperatures (Q > 300). The perfect B1 lattice barely dissipates pure shear at these frequencies.
- **Bridgmanite:** $Q^{-1}\propto T^{1.0}$ in both channels, the classical anharmonic result.
- **Comparison at 5000 K, 1 THz:**
  - Shear: bridgmanite $Q^{-1}$ = 0.016; MgO C₄₄ < 0.003. Bridgmanite is lossier, through its internal-strain (octahedral) coupling (Eq. 14 of the summary).
  - Uniaxial: bridgmanite 0.016; MgO 0.037. MgO is lossier.

- **Against MgO aged Schottky** (V6.6868, 5000 K): Q = 9.7 at 50 GHz and 3.7 at 20 GHz. Bridgmanite with V_Mg + V_O is at least 10–35× less lossy at the same frequencies.

- **O vacancies are fast and consistent with a single activation energy.**
  - Eq. 2 gives E = 2.36 ± 0.12 eV with χ²/ν = 0.6.
  - The local energies are 1.8 ± 0.8 eV (3000–4000 K) and 3.3 ± 1.2 eV (4000–5000 K). They differ by 1σ, so curvature is neither shown nor excluded.

- **Si moves at 5000 K** (MSD 2.7 Å² over 1.5 ns) although the cell has no Si vacancies. This points to Si jumping into Mg vacancies (antisites) or thermal Frenkel defects. The aging also showed a −6 eV PE settling at 5000 K. **Not yet checked:** a site-occupancy analysis of the 5000 K trajectory.

**Temperature series (8×8×8 cell).** G″ at 20 GHz rises 6.5× from 4000 to 5000 K at constant G′. If the 20 GHz points are on the high-frequency flank of one relaxation (G″ ≈ Δ/ωτ, so G″ ∝ 1/τ), a weighted Arrhenius fit of ln G″ against 1/k_BT gives

$$E_{app}=3.2\pm0.6\ \text{eV}\quad(\chi^2=0.2;\ \text{local } 3.8\ \text{and}\ 2.6\ \text{eV}) \tag{7}$$

within the range of the MgO vacancy-hop energies from MD (Mg 3.6 → 2.1 eV, O 4.6 → 2.1 eV over 3500–5000 K, summary §5a).

**Cell-size test: neither prediction holds.** For a cell-scale diffusional relaxation (τ ∝ L²) the 6×6×6 cell should show 1.78× the reference G″ (≈ 68 GPa at 20 GHz, ≈ 24 GPa at 50 GHz); for a local mechanism, the same G″. Measured: 1.1 ± 1.5 GPa at 20 GHz (35× less) and 7.6 ± 0.6 GPa at 50 GHz (0.56×). G′ is also 11–22 GPa lower (119–120 against 131–141 GPa) and flat. In the small cell G″ *falls* toward low frequency, the signature of the low-frequency flank (ωτ ≲ 1, relaxed G′), whereas the reference rises toward low frequency (high-frequency flank). Read literally, τ(6³) is at least 10× shorter than τ(8³), far more than (8/6)² = 1.78.
- **Stationarity:** the small cell is stationary (PE drift < 1.5 eV, p constant to 0.1 GPa over 2 ns). The reference 20 GHz run (1 ns) shows block-to-block pressure jumps of up to 1.6 GPa and PE steps of ±1 eV, i.e. slow configurational events on the 100 ps scale that the 18-cycle lock-in cannot separate from the drive. The large-cell 20 GHz G″ values (and therefore Eq. 7) may contain such event noise.
- **Defect state differs:** 300 ps NVT ageing from freshly placed bound pairs (small cell) against 1.5 ns NVE ageing (reference). If the loss comes from a cluster or aggregate that forms only after long ageing (or needs ≥ 20 pairs), the small cell would not show it.
- **Conclusion:** the 20 GHz MgO Schottky loss is not established as a cell-scale diffusional relaxation, and so cannot yet be mapped onto grain size. Its T dependence (Eq. 7) is consistent with vacancy hopping, but the size test contradicts both simple models.
- **Tests that would decide:** (i) repeat the 8×8×8 at 20 GHz for 2 ns (40 cycles), new seed: **submitted 11-16, job 6963124** (`5000K/FreqSweep_Schottky_aged_2ns`), and the same at γ₀ = 0.01 for linearity: **job 6965639_2** (RC 11-16); (ii) a second 6×6×6 with 1.5 ns ageing before the sweep: **submitted 11-16, job 6963125** (`5000K/FreqSweep_Schottky_L6_aged`); both dump the trajectory every 10 ps for (iii) a vacancy-cluster / event analysis against the stress record.

READMEs: `5000K/FreqSweep_Schottky_aged_2ns` (the output of 6963124 is in the run root) and `5000K/FreqSweep_Schottky_L6_aged`. Vacancy analysis: new `tools/b1_vacancy_clusters.py` on the trajectories (every 10 ps; rigid drift removed). Output `vac_clusters.txt` in each run dir.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8×8×8, 20 pairs, new seed, 2 ns | 1.5 ns NVE (as the reference) | 143.7 ± 3.5 | **27.3 ± 2.4** | 5.3 | 0.027, 0.021 | **one cluster of 40–42 vacancies** (all of them) |

| 6×6×6, 8 pairs | **1.5 ns NVT** | 118.6 ± 0.7 | **2.3 ± 0.5** | 51 | 0.008, 0.014 | **one cluster of 16 vacancies** (all of them) |
| 6×6×6 (6929874) | 300 ps NVT | 118.7 ± 1.1 | 1.1 ± 1.5 | 107 | – | (no trajectory) |

- **The large-cell loss is reproducible.** The new 2 ns run gives 27.3 ± 2.4 GPa against 38 ± 9 GPa, within 1.2σ; the weighted mean is 28 ± 2 GPa. Event noise does not explain it.
- **Ageing does not explain the small-cell result.** With 1.5 ns of ageing the 6×6×6 cell still has G″ = 2.3 ± 0.5 GPa, 12× below the 8×8×8 cell, and the same flat G′ ≈ 119 GPa.
- **The defect state is not point vacancies.** In both cells every vacancy belongs to one compact cluster for the whole run: a nanovoid of 40 sites (r ≈ 4.0 Å) in the 8×8×8 cell and 16 sites (r ≈ 2.9 Å) in the 6×6×6 cell. There is no slip (z-slab offsets ≤ 0.3 Å) and no extra disorder (≈ 40–50 atoms > 1 Å off site, as thermal).
  - That is the expected fate of 1 % vacancies, about 10⁶ times the equilibrium concentration at 5000 K (A1).
  - The 20 GHz "Schottky loss" is therefore the anelasticity of a nanovoid in a periodic cell, and its "cell-size" dependence is a void-size dependence.
- **A single Debye relaxation fits the 20 GHz pair.** Take the relaxed modulus G_R ≈ 119 GPa (6×6×6, flat) and the unrelaxed modulus G_U ≈ 171 GPa (perfect-crystal C₄₄ at 0.2 THz, §3.1 of the summary); then Δ ≈ 52 GPa.

$$G'=G_R+\Delta\frac{\omega^2\tau^2}{1+\omega^2\tau^2},\qquad G''=\Delta\frac{\omega\tau}{1+\omega^2\tau^2} \tag{8}$$

  - 8×8×8: G′ = 143.7 gives ωτ ≈ 0.96 (τ ≈ 7.6 ps) and predicts G″ ≈ 26 GPa; measured 27.3.
  - 6×6×6: G″(50 GHz) = 7.6 gives ωτ ≈ 0.15 (τ ≈ 0.5 ps) and predicts G″(20 GHz) ≈ 3 GPa; measured 2.3 ± 0.5.
  - The two τ differ by ≈ 16× for 2.5× more vacancies in the void.
  - The same Debye law does not fit the 8×8×8 50 GHz point (it predicts G′ ≈ 163 against 131 GPa measured), so there is a distribution of τ.
  - **The relaxation strength (≈ 30 % of G) is far larger than any point-defect relaxation at 1 % concentration.** It belongs to the void and its surface.
- **Conclusion:** the MgO "Schottky" loss below 50 GHz (Eq. 7, E_app = 3.2 eV, and summary §3.1) comes from vacancy aggregates (nanovoids) formed at a 10⁶-fold supersaturation. It cannot be carried to dispersed equilibrium vacancies, nor mapped onto grain size. It is not a candidate seismic-band mechanism without a physical source of voids.
- **Linearity (6965639_2, γ₀ = 0.01, 2 ns; analysed 2026-11-17):** G″ = 25.2 ± 5.0 GPa against 27.3 ± 2.4 at γ₀ = 0.02 (ratio 0.92 ± 0.20), harmonics 4.1 % / 2.4 %. The loss is linear in amplitude, so it is not strain-triggered. G′ = 127 ± 5 against 144 ± 3.5 GPa (2.8σ lower; one run each). The same single 40-site void is present throughout (`P50ps_xz_g001/vac_clusters.txt`). A run with dispersed vacancies at a realistic concentration is impossible in MD (too few defects). Point-defect anelasticity must come from the defect rates instead (A1–A3, plan Eqs. 6–8).

## 3. Answer

1. **Liquids are activated but not single-E.** MgSiO₃ is strongly super-Arrhenius ($E_{loc}$ for η from 1.6 to 3.6 eV; VFT $T_0$ ≈ 2800 K). MgO is mildly curved (1.3 to 1.8 eV).
2. **Phonon loss in both perfect crystals is not activated.**
   - At 1 THz, $Q^{-1}\propto T^{1.0\text{–}1.2}$ for both perfect crystals.
   - At 0.2 THz, MgO uniaxial loss is T-independent.
   - The formal energies (0.2–0.4 eV) are far below any barrier.
   - The same holds for boundary-rich MgO at 18 GPa (T-independent shear $Q^{-1}$, §5j of the summary).
3. **Activated processes exist only in the defects, and their energies differ between the two minerals.**
   - O-vacancy jumps in bridgmanite are consistent with a single E ≈ 2.4 eV (isochoric) over 3000–5000 K.
   - Mg-vacancy jumps in bridgmanite are frozen below 5000 K on ns scales.
   - MgO vacancy jumps are curved (§5a).
   - At THz none of these defects adds loss resolvable above the phonon background in bridgmanite.
   - The seismic-band question therefore rests on the low-frequency defect relaxation. For MgO it is seen below 50 GHz. **For bridgmanite with V_Mg + V_O it is not resolved down to 20 GHz (Q ≥ 65–280; 2026-11-15).**
