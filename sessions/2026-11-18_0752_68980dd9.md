This is the self-contained guide for repeating the MgO work at another volume or for another material. It covers the physics, the protocol that worked, every tool, the cost and the pitfalls. Dated restart docs (`ANELASTICITY_RESTART_<date>.md`) hold the run-by-run history and job IDs. `REQUIREMENTS.md` holds the user's standing rules. ## 1. What is measured

The goal is a deformation's complex modulus over 3–5 decades of frequency, plus the zero-frequency viscosities. Two independent routes are used:

1. **Oscillatory NEMD.** `fix deform wiggle` imposes the strain, and a lock-in fit is made at the known drive frequency.
2. **Green–Kubo (GK).** Equilibrium NVE stress autocorrelations.

For the liquid they must agree at overlapping frequencies (they do for MgO). For the crystal, only the sweep gives the elastic storage modulus.

### 1.1 Definitions

Shear (xz tilt; strain = tilt/$L_z$; stress $\sigma_{xz}=-P_{xz}$):

$$\gamma(t)=\gamma_0\sin\omega t,\qquad \sigma(t)=\gamma_0\left[G'(\omega)\sin\omega t+G''(\omega)\cos\omega t\right] \tag{1}$$

$$G^*=G'+iG'',\qquad Q^{-1}=\tan\delta=\frac{G''}{G'},\qquad \eta'(\omega)=\frac{G''}{\omega} \tag{2}$$

Uniaxial (x length; strain $\delta L_x/L_x$) measures the longitudinal modulus:

$$M^*=K^*+\tfrac43G^*,\qquad \frac{M''}{\omega}\xrightarrow{\omega\to0}\zeta_0+\tfrac43\eta_0 \tag{3}$$

Viscosity is the low-frequency slope of the loss modulus:

$$\eta_0=\lim_{\omega\to0}\frac{dG''}{d\omega} \tag{4}$$

It is estimated three ways:
- secants between sweep points;
- a weighted fit through the origin for $f\le200$ GHz;
- the analytic GK derivative $\int G(t)[\cos\omega t-\omega t\sin\omega t]\,dt$.

$Q$ has special cases:
- **Liquid-like points:** if $|G''|>|G'|$, $Q=G'/G''$ is computed directly.
- **Unresolved points:** reported as 2σ bounds ("$Q>$" or "$Q<$").
- **GK $Q$:** given only for liquid shear. The GK storage modulus lacks $K_0$ (longitudinal) and the Born term (crystal).

## 0. Idea

NEMD and Green–Kubo stop near 1 GHz. Equilibrium kinetic Monte Carlo reaches lower frequencies, but only for events it can find. ### Claude (11-18 08:10)

I've written the plan in `ANELASTICITY_PLAN_FE.md`. Nothing is submitted yet. It follows the MgO/MgSiO₃ program: Q from oscillatory shear and uniaxial runs on perfect, defect and polycrystalline cells, plus effective viscosity from vacancy diffusion, dislocation drag and grain-boundary sliding, extrapolated to seismic frequencies with the grain-scale model.

**Two problems to fix before any production run**
1. **The current force field has no hcp in it.** All 913 training configurations are bcc or liquid, and 350 GPa is at or past the edge. A round of VASP 6.6.1 on-the-fly training on hcp cells comes first, and it is the biggest new cost.
2. **Electronic temperature.** At 5000–7000 K the forces depend on the electronic temperature set in DFT. The INCARs in the refit directory use 0.26 eV (3000 K) and 0.13 eV (1500 K), not k_BT at 5000–7000 K. A VASP force field has no temperature input, unlike Deng's DeePMD model. The first test checks whether one force field at 6000 K is good enough.

**Other differences from MgO**
- hcp is anisotropic, so four drives are needed: xz shear (C₄₄), xy shear (C₆₆), uniaxial along a (C₁₁) and along c (C₃₃). Aggregate Q comes from a Voigt average of the complex constants (Eqs. 2–3).
- c/a changes with T, so each (P, T) cell is first equilibrated at constant pressure with c/a free. The MgO approach of one fixed cubic volume for all T doesn't carry over.
- The hcp atom site has no inversion centre. As in bridgmanite, strain couples to an optic mode (E₂g), but only in the basal channels. So crystal Q is quoted at ≤ 1 THz.
- In ~30 Å cells the lowest standing wave is near 1.2 THz, so the 5 THz points are not usable, as in MgO.
- Temperatures are set as fractions of the model's own melting point at 330 GPa (0.70, 0.80, 0.90, 0.95, 0.98 T_m), because that controls defects, premelting and diffusion. 330 GPa is the main pressure, with 360 GPa at two temperatures.

**Phases (cost in node-hours on 64-core icelake nodes)**

| phase | content | node-h |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | DFT settings (k-points, cutoff), electronic-temperature test, timing | ≈ 15 |
| A1 | on-the-fly training: hcp at 320/360 GPa × 3 T, vacancy, stacking fault, liquid | ≈ 200–400 |
| A2–A3 | refit, held-out DFT test, then melting point by hcp–liquid coexistence, C_ij(T), liquid viscosity vs Alfè et al. 2000, E₂g frequency | ≈ 110 |
| B | Q runs on perfect hcp, melt-grown polycrystal, vacancies, {10-12} twin and Σ7 tilt boundaries | ≈ 730 |
| C | vacancy formation and migration free energies, diffusion and creep viscosities, dislocation drag, grain-scale Q at 1 Hz | ≈ 175 |
| **total** | | **≈ 1250–1550** |

A reduced first pass of about 650 node-hours answers the main question: Q against T/T_m and the diffusion-creep viscosity. After A3 there is a go/no-go point: production starts only if the model's melting point, elastic constants and liquid viscosity fall within the published spread.

**What I recommend:** keep a DeePMD option open at that go/no-go point. The same DFT data could train a DeePMD model with temperature as an input, as Deng's MgSiO₃ model has. That fixes the electronic-temperature problem and runs on P100s. MgO-type speeds would make phases B and C about 4× cheaper, but that speed for Fe is not measured yet.

The MPI × OpenMP scans (7166452 on CPU, 7166454 on H200) are still pending. The costs above assume 6.5 steps/s per 8192 atoms on one node, scaled linearly to other cell sizes, and will be rescaled once the scans finish.

**Decisions for you (plan §8)**
1. hcp only, or bcc too? The existing force field was fitted to bcc, and a stable, fast-diffusing bcc phase would change the viscosity by orders of magnitude.
2. If the A0 test shows electronic-temperature sensitivity: one force field per temperature band, or the DeePMD route?
3. Full plan (≈ 1300–1550 node-hours) or the reduced first pass (≈ 650)?
4. 330 GPa as the main pressure?
5. Light elements and superionic alloys: a later phase?

If you approve, the first step is A0, about 15 node-hours on one 180-atom hcp cell, which sets the training cost. Literature values marked (†) in the plan are from memory and need checking against the papers before they are quoted. `INPROGRESS.md` §3 and `ANELASTICITY_TODO.md` point to the plan.
