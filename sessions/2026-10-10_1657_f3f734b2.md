def msd(u, lags):
    return np.array([np.mean((u[L:] - u[:-L]) ** 2) for L in lags])

def analyse(run):

        fit = (lags * dt >= 0.1 * t[-1]) & (lags * dt <= 0.33 * t[-1])
        c = np.polyfit(lags[fit] * dt, m[fit], 1)

### Claude (10-10 17:24)

They are not tied to a drive frequency. These are equilibrium runs with no applied strain, and β comes from how far the grains wander in the MSD (Eq. 12). In principle that is the zero-frequency limit. In practice a finite run only probes frequencies down to roughly 1/(2π × lag).

| lag window | frequencies it probes |
|---|---|
| 2–10 ps | ~16–80 GHz |
| 5–20 ps | ~8–30 GHz |
| 10–40 ps | ~4–16 GHz |
| 25–80 ps (the main fit uses about 25–100 ps) | ~2–6 GHz |

So the β ≈ 2–10×10⁶ Pa s/m values are effectively **GHz-band** friction. The long-lag value of 5–8×10⁶ belongs to about 2–6 GHz, and the short-lag value of about 2×10⁶ to tens of GHz. This is the same band as the forced sweeps (20–50 GHz and 0.2 THz). It also explains why the equilibrium cell viscosity of 27 mPa s is of the same order as the 17 mPa s from the 0.2 THz sweep.

**Seismic frequencies (mHz–Hz) are 9–12 orders lower, and β is not shown to be converged there.**
- The windowed β rises steadily with lag (lower frequency means higher friction), and it has not levelled off by 80 ps.
- If sliding is pinned at long times (shear-coupled migration with obstacles, or the elastic limit of the flat region in §5e), β(ω) keeps growing as ω → 0. At the extreme the boundary is effectively locked at seismic frequencies.
- So the size test shows that the GHz-band β is local and transferable across area. It does not give the seismic-band β.
- Plugging the GHz β straight into `gb_grain_model.py` would underestimate the friction, and would put the loss peak at too high a frequency or too small a grain size.

**Ways to get the low-frequency β:**
1. **Longer runs (ns) at V9.6397 3000 K** to see whether D_u(t) reaches a constant (true diffusive sliding, finite β₀) or keeps falling (pinning). This is cheap for the 1× and 2×1 cells. I'd run 2–5 ns with a few replicas.
2. **Rates from the disconnection or coupled-migration NEB.** Harmonic TST gives the event rate directly, so it can be extrapolated to any frequency. This is the only route at 140 GPa, and it is the approach you prefer for testing a single activation energy.
